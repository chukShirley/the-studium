# One and Many: Pilot Protocol

## Purpose

This pilot asks whether one objective lesson can be mediated through Concrete, Discursive, and Sapiential presentations without confusing presentation difficulty with intellectual mastery.

It is a usability and curriculum study, not a clinical study, formal assessment, or evaluation of a learner's worth or general ability.

## Participants

Begin with five to eight voluntary participants representing several starting points:

- a younger learner who benefits from concrete language;
- an adolescent already familiar with classical education;
- an adult beginning liberal study;
- an adult comfortable with philosophical prose;
- a parent or tutor observing at least one learner.

For minors, a parent or guardian should be present and should decide whether participation is appropriate. Do not collect full names, birth dates, contact details, diagnoses, school records, or other sensitive information in the prototype.

## Before the lesson

1. Give the learner the deployed URL and no navigation instructions beyond “begin when you are ready.”
2. Explain that the prototype stores work only in that browser.
3. Ask the learner to choose a presentation based on what would help today, not age or perceived rank.
4. Record outside the application only a non-identifying participant code and broad prior experience, if needed.

## During the lesson

Observe without teaching unless the learner is genuinely blocked. Note:

- whether onboarding and register descriptions are understood;
- the first register chosen and the learner's reason;
- any register changes and what prompted them;
- words, instructions, or controls that cause hesitation;
- whether the learner speaks before writing when asked;
- whether the off-screen transition is taken seriously;
- whether the learner returns without being prompted by artificial rewards.

Do not equate fluent philosophical vocabulary with understanding. Attend to examples, distinctions, explanations, and transfer.

## Immediate conversation

After the lesson, ask without correcting first:

1. What was the lesson chiefly about?
2. What is a unit?
3. How can different things be counted together?
4. Is the written figure `5` the number itself? Explain.
5. Did your chosen presentation help? Where was it too simple or too demanding?

Invite the learner to complete the in-app reflection and copy its summary into the feedback channel established by the pilot organizer.

## Delayed recollection

Ask the learner to return after roughly one day and complete the recollection without reopening the lesson. Then ask for a new example, such as counting twelve eggs as twelve units or as one dozen.

The important evidence is not exact wording but whether the learner can retain and transfer the distinctions among the unit, the multitude numbered, and the sign of number.

## Guide review

Review the guide report with the learner where appropriate. Check whether its statements are supported by the recorded evidence and whether its language helps a real conversation. Record any place where the report claims more than the evidence warrants.

## Synthesis after the first round

For each participant, summarize:

- starting presentation and presentations encountered;
- observed procedural fluency;
- quality of conceptual distinction;
- delayed recall and transfer;
- usability obstacles;
- learner and guide judgments;
- curriculum or product changes indicated.

Revise the lesson and evidence rules before expanding the curriculum or implementing permanent learner accounts.
